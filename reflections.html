🌱 Ecosystem Studies (EST 1101)
Module Overview

Ecology, Evolution & Conservation Dynamics

Reflecting on structure-function relationships in ecosystem ecology, trophic energy transfers, and biogeochemical nutrient cycling.

1. Conceptual Understanding & Dynamics

Diversity & Richness

Diversity: Refers to species abundance and evenness.
Richness: Refers to the total number of distinct species in a given community.

Holdridge Life Zone & Classification

Global forest classifications utilize indices like the Holdridge Life Zone model, governed by Annual Precipitation, Humidity, and Temperature.

2. Evolution, Speciation & Relict Species

Speciation Mechanisms

Allopatric: Geographic barriers isolate populations.
Peripatric & Parapatric: Peripheral niches or adjacent borders.
Sympatric: Speciation within the same region via environmental gradients.

Types of Relicts

Taxonomic: Surviving lineages while relatives go extinct.
Geographic: Formerly widespread, now restricted.
Climatic: Persistence driven by microclimate controls.

3. Biogeography & Biodiversity Patterns

Continental Drift & Plate Tectonics

Wegener's drift theory and boundaries dictate global biodiversity distribution and mountain formation.

Environmental Stability & Gradients

Driven by environmental stability, establishing distinct horizontal and vertical species gradients.

4. Field Work Observations & Ecosystem Comparisons

Evaluating three distinct ecosystems, their adaptations, and environmental vulnerabilities:

A. Pashan Lake (Wetland & Aquatic Ecosystem)

An artificial wetland ecosystem characterized by shallow lentic waters and rich migratory bird habitats.

B. Mayureshwar Wildlife Sanctuary, Supe (Grassland Ecosystem)

Grassland with loose topsoil and basaltic bedrock resulting in poor moisture retention and soil erosion risks.

C. Mulshi (Montane Catchment & Forest Ecosystem)

Ecologically rich catchment area supplying vital water resources to the Mulshi Dam reservoir.

5. Group Work & Collaborative Food Web Dynamics

Modeled primary producer and consumer roles within a Desert Ecosystem group activity to evaluate food web interdependence.

6. Nilgiri Tahr: Ecosystem Presentation & Reflection

Analysis of montane grassland-shola ecosystems of the Western Ghats and conservation strategies for the endangered endemic Nilgiri Tahr.

REFLEXION 01

Assignment 1: Story Map

Narration: I had to prepare an analysis map for the interactive map section of story map to show the change in the forest cover and I had difficulty getting the proper data. So, I made a map with the help of the raster I had found but at the last moment I was able to visualize the data in a better manner.

Description: Initially the raster data I got was for the study area map but it was pixelated and somehow the data was not correctly illustrating the forest cover data I wanted to show through my map so, much to my dismay, I made the layout and had embedded it to my story map. Unexpectedly with the help of AI, I was able to get the forest cover per district of Jharkhand with which I could show not only the forest cover of 2023 but also the forest cover change from 2021 to 2023 without having the data of 2021 but with only with the change percentage given in the attribute table. With a little coding I was able to extract the data and was able to make not 1 but 3 maps, one for the study are and 2 for the forest cover change analysis which made the map visualization of my story map so much better.

Reflection: I was really surprised that I was able to pull this off and could produce high-quality maps, 2 of which are in the interactive section. It made me somehow feel accomplished and that day I understood the importance of coding in geospatial analysis. It also made me realize the importance of acquisition of the correct data for different needs as it was also taught in the class and this was playing in head on a loop. So, from next time I will be careful in selecting the correct data for visualization.
REFLEXION 02

Assignment 2: Field Survey

Narration: I faced issues with the accuracy of the location while collecting the location of the points for my field survey.

Description: I had previously made the mistake of not updating the location while collecting data in Pashan, so since then I have been very careful about the location accuracy unfortunately, I was facing with issues with the accuracy this time, with it going to 10 to 15, even after changing the position, making sure that there was no hinderance above when I could, it helped to some extent, a very few times I got lucky with the accuracy of 2 but I had to wait and walk around to get an of accuracy of 4-5, which was time consuming.

Reflection: Honestly, I have not come up with a proper solution to this problem, I am still working on it so that I don’t face any issue next time. In the class I got to learn later about more precautions to be taken which I will definitely use next time for the field survey.

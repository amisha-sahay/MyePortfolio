2. Evolution, Speciation & Relict Species

Speciation Mechanisms

Allopatric: Geographic barriers isolate populations.
Peripatric & Parapatric: Peripheral niches or adjacent borders.
Sympatric: Speciation within the same region via environmental gradients.

Types of Relicts

Taxonomic: Surviving lineages while relatives go extinct.
Geographic: Formerly widespread, now restricted.
Climatic: Persistence driven by microclimate controls.

5. Ecosystem Services & Valuation

Ecosystem services represent the multifaceted benefits that humans freely derive from natural ecosystems, bridging ecological functions with human well-being. To systematically evaluate these contributions, ecological assessments categorize services into four core pillars:

🍎
Provisioning

Food, fresh water, timber, and fiber production.

🐝
Regulating

Climate regulation, flood control, and water purification.

🌱
Supporting

Soil formation, nutrient cycling, and primary production.

🏞️
Cultural

Recreational, spiritual, and educational enrichment.

6. Community Interactions, Succession & Conservation Management

Looking back at how ecosystems function on a day-to-day basis, it becomes clear that nature is never static. Inter-specific competition, predator-prey dynamics, and mutualistic symbiosis constantly shape community structures, while keystone species act as the glue holding complex food webs together. When disturbances strike—whether natural or human-induced—ecosystems respond through primary and secondary succession, slowly rebuilding their resilience. However, given the unprecedented pressures of modern habitat fragmentation and climate change, relying solely on natural recovery is no longer enough. Effective conservation management requires a thoughtful blend of protected area design, safeguarding biodiversity hotspots, and implementing robust policy frameworks. True environmental stewardship means balancing active human resource use with long-term ecological preservation, ensuring that the natural systems supporting our survival are protected for generations to come.

7. Connecting Coursework to Sustainable Development Goals

When studying ecology and geoinformatics, it is easy to get lost in technical terms, mapping coordinates, and theoretical models. Realizing that our academic coursework directly feeds into these global mandates makes the science feel personal, urgent, and deeply meaningful.

9. Mangrove Loss, Coastal Vulnerability & AI Reliability

Research Question: Are the effects of mangrove loss on coastal vulnerability observed globally also evident in the Sundarbans, and do they act through trophic cascades?

Reflection on AI Reliability (Amisha Sahay)

AI has proved to be a very useful tool time and again, but at the same time, it has also shown us its inaccuracy at times. For a surface-level assessment, it has proven useful most of the time, but when we dive deeper into a topic, it may start to make up ‘facts’. Out of 12 total claims examined in our Sundarbans research, 6 were supported by scientific literature while the remaining 6 were only partially true, revealing the dual nature of AI. Even when AI provides a confident and well-structured answer, its reliability cannot be judged solely by how convincing the information sounds. As students of Geoinformatics, we must learn to use AI responsibly by cross-checking information against peer-reviewed research, consulting credible literature, and evaluating evidence before accepting any claim as scientifically valid.

📊 Statistical Methods (EST 1104)
Reflection Overview

The Art of Statistics

The Statistical Methods course started with the very basics of statistics which was its definition and its types: Descriptive and Inferential. In Descriptive Statistics, we describe the dataset with the help of measures of central tendency and dispersion, sampling and probability followed by graphical representation which helps us to determine any patterns or trends that is present. Whereas in Inferential Statistics we make conclusion or decisions after describing the data through various tests like hypothesis formulation, z-test, t-test, f-test, ANOVA, chi square test, correlation and regression.

Initially, I had difficulty understanding most of the course as I had very basic knowledge of just the descriptive statistics and 2-3 topics of inferential statistics, but after the practicals and going through the course on my own I finally understood the topics taught in the class. The practicals were helpful to understand the workings of the excel but I faced difficulties while performing the practical in the exam as it all got confusing at the end since there was no logical understanding of the excel functions which could have helped me to apply in the practical exam. Then there was lack of examples related to our field which was the major reason that I could not connect with the course most of the time and the other times the examples were so complex that understanding them was tough.

Conclusion & Takeaway: Overall, the course was a great learning curve as it taught me the importance of self-study and to not be hesitant in the class and to ask more questions, when in doubt. I truly understood the importance of the statistical methods while studying and its role especially in my thesis in the 3rd semester. So, I will try to work on my concepts to not repeat the mistakes I did in this semester.
📡 Fundamentals of Remote Sensing (GEO 1101)
Reflection Overview

The Foundation of Remote Sensing & Spatial Science

Fundamentals of Remote Sensing course is the first stepping stone in understanding the basic concepts, working and application of Remote Sensing. We were introduced to this course with the introduction of physics of Conduction, Convection and Radiation along with Electromagnetic Induction and its properties which were a refresher for me, therefore easing me into this course. Unit after unit as we progressed, I got to learn more about the science behind remote sensing, its origin, how much it has changed especially in the recent decades and how much it will change in the upcoming years.

Getting to know about the different bands and how different features react to different bands which helps us to study and analyze them was really interesting. A lot of this course was very briefly covered in my undergrad so understanding this course as a whole was new but familiar.

The activity which I enjoyed the most was the duo-activity we did on the very first day of our course. Then we divided ourselves in groups of 2 and were assigned different routes where we observed the built-features along our route and then map them on Google Earth Pro. As it was the first day, I was expecting a proper theory class but this outside the classroom activity was actually refreshing and a great way to start the semester by visual observation and understanding.

Conclusion: Everything learned in this course is going to be applied in my research, fieldwork and internship as it forms the very base of Geoinformatics so its application will be the foundation of various analysis.

🛰️ Techniques in Image Interpretation and Remote Sensing (GEO 1113)
Reflection Overview

Art of Image Interpretation and Science of Remote Sensing

I was honestly very curious as to what we were going to learn in this course and it has been pretty interesting to learn about different Image Interpretation Techniques in Remote Sensing. Initially I had learned the basic concepts of topography and its interpretation, but through this course, I got to learn beyond. With the practicals starting from basics of a Topography Sheet interpretation to digitizing a UAV image and its interpretation, this course taught me about satellite data acquisition through various platforms, map-making, topo sheet to image registration, image-to-image registration, working of different GIS software like ArcGIS Pro, QGIS and SAGA, identifying different features with the help of the 7 interpretation keys in both panchromatic and coloured images and making Land-Use Land Cover maps and analyzing the land area coverage by different land-cover types with proper data visualization. These practicals allowed me to explore the world of geoinformatics even more with a different point of view which broadened my perspective of the field.

The practical I enjoyed doing was the digitization of the UAV imagery. It was difficult to digitize it at the beginning as I was still learning about the various editing tools provided by the ArcGIS Pro but as I got a hang of it and seeing the final map felt very rewarding. While learning I had done a lot of mistakes which I could not rectify before the submission but re-doing it gave me the chance to properly analyze my mistakes and present it better by re-working of the polygons that were not digitized in the previous submission and adding their information and area calculation in the attribute table for interpretation. This practical was a really great enlightening experience in terms of discovering the ArcGIS software tools and their application.

Conclusion: Overall, Techniques in Image Interpretation and Remote Sensing has been a really intriguing course which enlightened me with both theoretical and practical knowledge that I can apply for various analysis in my fieldworks, research and internships.

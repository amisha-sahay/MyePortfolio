🌱 Ecosystem Studies (EST 1101) - Reflections
Module Overview

Ecology, Evolution & Conservation Dynamics

Reflecting on structure-function relationships in ecosystem ecology, trophic energy transfers, and biogeochemical nutrient cycling.

1. Conceptual Understanding & Dynamics

Diversity & Richness

Diversity: Refers to species abundance and evenness.
Richness: Refers to the total number of distinct species in a given community.

Holdridge Life Zone & Classification

Global forest classifications utilize indices like the Holdridge Life Zone model, governed by Annual Precipitation, Humidity, and Temperature.

2. Evolution, Speciation & Relict Species

Speciation Mechanisms

Allopatric: Geographic barriers isolate populations.
Peripatric & Parapatric: Peripheral niches or adjacent borders.
Sympatric: Speciation within the same region via environmental gradients.

Types of Relicts

Taxonomic: Surviving lineages while relatives go extinct.
Geographic: Formerly widespread, now restricted.
Climatic: Persistence driven by microclimate controls.

3. Biogeography & Biodiversity Patterns

Continental Drift & Plate Tectonics

Wegener's drift theory and boundaries dictate global biodiversity distribution and mountain formation.

Environmental Stability & Gradients

Driven by environmental stability, establishing distinct horizontal and vertical species gradients.

4. Field Work Observations & Ecosystem Comparisons

Evaluating three distinct ecosystems, their adaptations, and environmental vulnerabilities:

A. Pashan Lake (Wetland & Aquatic Ecosystem)

An artificial wetland ecosystem characterized by shallow lentic waters and rich migratory bird habitats.

B. Mayureshwar Wildlife Sanctuary, Supe (Grassland Ecosystem)

Grassland with loose topsoil and basaltic bedrock resulting in poor moisture retention and soil erosion risks.

C. Mulshi (Montane Catchment & Forest Ecosystem)

Ecologically rich catchment area supplying vital water resources to the Mulshi Dam reservoir.

5. Group Work & Collaborative Food Web Dynamics

Modeled primary producer and consumer roles within a Desert Ecosystem group activity to evaluate food web interdependence.

6. Nilgiri Tahr: Ecosystem Presentation & Reflection

Analysis of montane grassland-shola ecosystems of the Western Ghats and conservation strategies for the endangered endemic Nilgiri Tahr.

🛰️ Fundamentals of Geospatial Science and Technology (GEO 1102) - Reflections
Module Overview

Learning to See Beyond the Map

Exploring how GIS, cartography, field observations, and critical thinking shape our understanding of spatial information.

REFLECTION 01

The Hidden Monsoon Jewel

Creating my StoryMap helped explore the relationship between Rugda mushrooms, Sal forests, and local livelihoods in Jharkhand, combining thematic maps with narrative design.

What changed? I began to see cartography as a medium for communicating spatial relationships rather than just displaying static data.
REFLECTION 02

From Field Observations to Spatial Data

Field data collection via Epicollect and GPS introduced systematic recording standards, emphasizing that attribute accuracy underpins reliable spatial analytics.

What changed? I developed a rigorous focus on location accuracy and data validation during early field workflows.
REFLECTION 03

Questioning AI-Generated Information

Evaluating AI-generated claims against scientific literature regarding mangrove loss revealed critical gaps, demonstrating why automated outputs require rigorous vetting.

What changed? I treat AI as a supporting tool rather than a substitute for verified scientific literature and critical thinking.
REFLECTION 04

Learning Through Challenges

Recognized that software outputs are not automatically correct. Coordinate systems, data quality, and classification choices demand continuous logical verification.

My next step: Systematically document intermediate steps and verify logical geographic consistency in all analyses.
